Case study · 01

Dividend &
Distribution
Payments

Company
Registry Direct
Domain
FinTech · Share registry
Role
Product designer

Context

Registry Direct is a share registry platform where companies and trusts self-manage their shareholder registers.

Tags

FinTechPaymentsTax logic

What we were building

Companies and trusts regularly pay shareholders a percentage of their earnings. The goal was to let clients calculate each individual payment per shareholder — automating a process that had previously required spreadsheets and manual bank transfers.

The calculation at its simplest: shares owned × price per unit = payment amount. But the real complexity lives in everything surrounding that formula.

A "payment cycle" spans the entire journey from setup to bank transfer — full of variables that differ per investor, per jurisdiction, and per entity type.

Some of the challenges

Tax withholding was a major complexity. How much to withhold depends on each shareholder's country of residence and whether they've supplied a TFN. If a shareholder doesn't have a bank account on file, do we send a cheque, or withhold entirely? All these variables had to be configurable by our clients.

The biggest challenge was extending this to trusts paying distributions. Trusts define each component of a payment separately, and each component carries different tax properties. We brought in Ernst & Young to map every possible component — their output was essentially dense spreadsheets we had to translate into UI rules.

Design and build process

We started with company dividends only — a more contained problem with fewer unknowns. Once we'd worked through that end-to-end and felt confident in the model, we applied the learnings to trust distributions.

Since this was a high-stakes feature, we released it first to a designated set of clients to gather feedback and iron out edge cases. We also ran training sessions with major clients — which surfaced assumptions in our design we hadn't anticipated.

Case study · 02

AML &
KYC

Company
Registry Direct
Domain
Compliance · Identity
Role
Product designer

Context

Under Australian law, companies and trusts must verify the identity of shareholders before accepting investments.

Tags

ComplianceGreenIDAPI

What we were building

Registry Direct holds a database of individuals, companies, and trusts with all their associated details. Companies on the platform are legally required to identify potential risks before accepting investments — and the process was entirely manual, managed outside the platform via document submissions.

Our clients needed a streamlined way to verify new shareholders — without running a separate compliance workflow outside the platform.

Some of the challenges

We evaluated multiple providers before settling on GreenID by GBG — the most stable API with the best documentation. But GreenID only covers Australian and NZ investors, so we needed a fallback for international shareholders.

Given the low volume of international investors, we decided a manual verification path — scanned documents — was defensible, even though some clients pushed for a fully electronic solution. The cost of a second API for a small subset was hard to justify.

Verification failures added another design challenge. They can be legitimate (a name that genuinely doesn't match) or benign (a recently changed address). The design had to give clients context to make a judgement call, and a path to manually override or re-verify.

Design and build process

We deliberately waited on prototyping until we fully understood what the GreenID API could and couldn't do. Once we had a clear picture, we built prototypes and ran facilitated sessions with a select group of clients — surfacing edge cases around partial verification states we hadn't anticipated.

Result

Clients can now manage all AML and CTF requirements within Registry Direct prior to accepting investments — compliant with Australian law, with no need to manage multiple systems.

Case study · 03

Capital
Raising

Company
Registry Direct
Domain
Investment · Form builder
Role
Product designer

Context

Companies and trusts regularly release new investment offerings. Historically managed via PDF forms and manual payment coordination.

Tags

Form builderWhite-labelInvestor UX

What we were building

Clients regularly release new investment offerings to the public. The old process: create a PDF describing the opportunity, attach a paper form, then manually reconcile investments with bank transfers.

Since our clients' investors were already on the platform, we had an opportunity to replace that workflow with a digital application form handling investment amounts, investor details, and payment in one place.

The goal: a white-label form builder where clients had full control — branding, rules, minimums — without needing us for every change request.

Some of the challenges

Our first iteration generated forms on demand — a client asked, we built it and shared the URL. This was unsustainable. Change requests were too frequent and too fiddly to manage at scale.

Handling existing investors was the more significant challenge. Most people investing in a raise were already shareholders. Allowing them to apply without creating duplicate records required careful identity matching logic, which shaped how we designed the investor-facing flow.

Payment management added complexity — clients needed to mark when money arrived, reissue invoices, and generate receipts. We had to design a back-office workflow that matched how these clients actually operated.

Design and build process

We ran extensive consultations before designing anything. The variety of ways different companies run capital raises was wider than we'd assumed — some required strict minimums, others soft; some collected payment upfront, others invoiced later.

That variety drove the decision to build a form builder rather than fixed templates. We used Rubix as the underlying framework and iterated heavily on the builder UI — the first version was powerful but intimidating. Subsequent rounds focused on reducing cognitive load for first-time users.
